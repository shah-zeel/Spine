## Inspiration

We wanted to explore how a foldable phone’s different screens and postures could make reading feel more natural and focused.

## What it does

Spine adapts the book to the device: a cover when closed, page layouts when open, notes beside the book, and a focused listening view in tent posture. Readers can highlight, take notes, listen to narration, and get a summary shaped by their annotations.

## How we built it

We built Spine in SwiftUI. It responds to orientation and hinge posture, with page-turn reading, synchronized narration, locally saved notes and highlights, and optional on-device summaries.

## Challenges we ran into

The main challenge was keeping the reading experience comfortable as the screen arrangement changes, while making each posture useful.

## Accomplishments that we're proud of

One app now supports several ways to read: a closed cover, open pages, a two-page spread, reading with notes, and tent-style listening.

## What we learned

The device’s form can shape how people read. Using different screens for the book, notes, or narration can make reading feel more flexible.

## What's next for Spine

We’d like to try Spine on more screen sizes, refine its reading layouts, and add more books.
